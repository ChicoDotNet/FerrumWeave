# FerrumWeave Brand Direction

FerrumWeave should feel **strong enough to carry critical systems and open enough to invite a community to build them together**.

This document records the visual direction chosen during the project's architectural-discovery phase. It is intentionally a design contract, not a finished corporate identity manual. Production SVG masters, exact typography licenses, accessibility validation, and final trademark work remain future deliverables.

## North star

**Ferrum Ox + the woven ring** is the primary visual metaphor.

The Ferrum Ox represents strength, service, patience, infrastructure, and the ability to carry real work. The woven ring represents interoperability: distinct strands becoming one dependable structure without losing their identity.

The preferred composition is the ox physically carrying or supporting the woven ring — the **Ox & Woven Ring** concept. It expresses the product thesis better than either symbol alone:

> **Built to carry critical systems. Woven to interoperate.**

The ox should feel steadfast rather than aggressive. It is a working animal, not a Wall Street bull, a minotaur, a war machine, or a symbol of domination.

## Brand character

FerrumWeave is:

- engineered, but human;
- industrial, but not dystopian;
- serious, but not intimidating;
- technically ambitious, but honest about evidence;
- open source, community-first, and trust-oriented;
- compatible with enterprise software without looking like generic enterprise software.

A useful shorthand is **"not evil" infrastructure**: trustworthy, legible, welcoming, and built in public.

## Color strategy

The approved hero artwork may use a deep charcoal / near-black environment because it works well in both light and dark GitHub themes and gives the metallic artwork the contrast it needs.

That does **not** make FerrumWeave a permanently dark UI brand.

For websites, documentation, tools, dashboards, and community surfaces:

- prefer clean Bootstrap/Fluent-style light and dark surfaces;
- use whitespace generously;
- keep text contrast high and interfaces calm;
- use rust/copper as an accent, not as a wall of color;
- use steel/graphite neutrals for structure;
- support dark mode, but do not make the project feel sinister, cyberpunk, militarized, or security-theater driven.

Current working palette from the concept boards:

| Role | Working color | Intent |
|---|---|---|
| Dark steel | `#1F2328` | deep neutral, hero/background accents |
| Graphite / oxidized iron | `#3C3F42`–`#4A525A` | structural neutral |
| Copper rust | `#B7410E` family | Rust/Ferrum accent and emphasis |
| Light steel | `#C7CDD1` | metallic secondary |
| Steel white | `#F4F5F6` | clean community/product surfaces |

These values are working tokens, not yet a final accessibility-certified palette.

## Marks and symbols

### Ferrum Ox

Primary mascot. Use it when the project benefits from personality, storytelling, community identity, conference presence, or merchandise.

### Woven ring

Primary compact emblem. It should become the basis for favicon, app icon, badges, stickers, and small-scale identity once a production vector is finalized.

### Ox + woven ring

Preferred narrative mark. The ox carrying the ring is the strongest expression of FerrumWeave's philosophy and should guide future hero art and major community illustrations.

### Wordmark

`FerrumWeave` should remain highly legible. The working visual system distinguishes **Ferrum** with steel/dark neutrals and **Weave** with rust/copper, or reverses emphasis where contrast requires it.

## Interface language

FerrumWeave's website and future tooling should use familiar, trustworthy interaction patterns rather than ornamental sci-fi controls.

- **Bootstrap** may define grid, layout rhythm, responsive containers, and predictable spacing.
- **Fluent UI 2** may define interactive controls, states, accessibility behavior, and theming.
- FerrumWeave's custom brand layer supplies color, typography, illustration, icons, and metallic/weave motifs.

Where framework defaults conflict with the brand, preserve usability first and customize through supported tokens rather than brittle CSS overrides.

## What to avoid

Do not drift toward:

- generic `.NET purple/blue` as the dominant identity;
- generic Bootstrap marketing pages with a logo pasted on top;
- permanent black-on-black cyberpunk interfaces;
- aggressive bull/minotaur imagery;
- flames, skulls, weapons, hacker clichés, or "dangerous" aesthetics;
- fake claims that FerrumWeave already delivers capabilities still on the roadmap;
- decorative complexity that makes the project less accessible.
