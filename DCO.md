# Developer Certificate of Origin

FerrumWeave uses the **Developer Certificate of Origin (DCO) 1.1** sign-off model for contributions.

The canonical DCO is maintained at:

https://developercertificate.org/

The DCO is a lightweight way for contributors to certify that they have the right to submit their work to the project under its open-source terms.

## How to sign off

Add a `Signed-off-by` trailer to each commit you contribute:

```text
Signed-off-by: Your Name <you@example.com>
```

Git can add this automatically:

```bash
git commit -s
```

Your sign-off should use a name and email address that you are authorized to use for the contribution.

## What the sign-off means

By signing off, you certify the contribution under the DCO 1.1 and agree that the contribution may be distributed under FerrumWeave's project licenses:

```text
MIT OR Apache-2.0
```

The sign-off is not a transfer of copyright to the project founder or to a company.

## Why FerrumWeave uses DCO

FerrumWeave is being structured so that, if a real multi-company and multi-community ecosystem develops, the project can evolve toward neutral governance without first untangling an opaque contribution history.

A clear provenance trail helps future maintainers understand who contributed work and under what certification.

## Automated enforcement

The repository may add an automated DCO check as contribution volume grows. Until then, maintainers should verify sign-off during review and help first-time contributors correct missing trailers without treating the process as a trap.
