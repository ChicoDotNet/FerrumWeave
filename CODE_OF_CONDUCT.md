# Code of Conduct

FerrumWeave is building a bridge between two mature technical ecosystems. That only works if the community itself is capable of the same kind of interoperability.

Our standard is simple: **technical rigor and human dignity are both non-negotiable.**

## Expected behavior

Contributors, maintainers, reviewers, users, and visitors are expected to:

- treat people with respect, patience, and professional courtesy;
- critique ideas, code, APIs, tests, and evidence rather than the person presenting them;
- assume good faith while still asking for evidence when claims matter;
- make room for differences in experience, native language, communication style, and technical background;
- give context when using ecosystem-specific terminology;
- accept constructive correction and update a position when better evidence appears;
- credit prior work, upstream projects, and sources appropriately;
- keep disagreement proportional to the technical decision being discussed;
- protect private or confidential information encountered through project work.

## Rust + .NET friendly means no language wars

FerrumWeave exists because both Rust and .NET have valuable ideas worth preserving.

Discussions about ownership, borrowing, garbage collection, the CLR, CTS, Cargo, MSBuild, NuGet, traits, interfaces, `Result`, exceptions, async models, ABI boundaries, or tooling should be framed as engineering trade-offs and compatibility questions.

Statements such as “Rust developers just need to learn how .NET works” or “.NET is legacy and should get out of Rust's way” are not useful design arguments here.

A contributor should be able to arrive from either ecosystem without being treated as a guest in the other's project.

## Unacceptable behavior

The following are not acceptable in FerrumWeave spaces:

- harassment, threats, intimidation, stalking, or sustained unwanted attention;
- discriminatory or demeaning conduct directed at a person or group;
- personal attacks, insults, ridicule, or deliberately hostile review behavior;
- sexualized content or attention in project spaces;
- publishing private information without permission;
- impersonation or deceptive identity practices intended to evade moderation;
- knowingly presenting copied or generated work as original without appropriate attribution;
- repeated disruption of technical discussion after maintainers have asked that behavior to stop;
- using project channels primarily for unsolicited commercial promotion, political campaigning, or unrelated advocacy.

Strong technical disagreement is allowed. Hostility toward people is not.

## Scope

This policy applies to repository discussions, issues, pull requests, reviews, project documentation, official community channels, project events, and situations where someone is explicitly representing FerrumWeave.

## Reporting a problem

Do not open a public issue containing sensitive personal information or details that could expose someone to additional harm.

Until FerrumWeave has a dedicated moderation address, contact a current maintainer privately using a contact method published on that maintainer's GitHub profile. If a safe private channel is not available, report the behavior to the platform hosting the interaction and avoid publishing sensitive details publicly.

Reports will be handled as privately as practical. Maintainers may request additional context, preserve relevant evidence, and take proportionate action to protect the community.

## Enforcement

Depending on severity, context, and prior behavior, maintainers may use one or more of the following responses:

1. clarification or informal correction;
2. a private or public warning;
3. temporary limits on participation;
4. temporary suspension from project spaces;
5. permanent removal from project spaces.

Serious incidents may skip intermediate steps.

Enforcement decisions should focus on protecting participants, restoring productive collaboration where possible, and applying standards consistently rather than winning an argument.

## A community design principle

FerrumWeave's visual language uses strength without aggression. The same principle applies to collaboration:

> **Be strong enough to challenge an idea and disciplined enough to respect the person who brought it.**
